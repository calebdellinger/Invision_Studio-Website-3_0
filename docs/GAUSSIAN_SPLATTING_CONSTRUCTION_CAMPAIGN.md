## The Big Idea

The construction industry is about to absorb hundreds of thousands of displaced white-collar workers — accountants, paralegals, analysts, middle managers — who have never stepped foot on a jobsite. These companies will need to onboard massive new crews fast, cheaply, and safely. Traditional training (PDFs, PowerPoints, generic YouTube videos, expensive Unity game-engine simulations) won't cut it.

**Invision Creative solves this with Gaussian Splatting** — photorealistic, interactive 3D captures of their *actual* jobsites, equipment, and hazard zones. Not a video game render. Their site. Their machines. Their real world.

## Campaign Pillars

### Pillar 1 — REAL WORLD, NOT RENDERED
*The jobsite is the training. Not a simulation of one.*

Every frame captured by a Sony A7R V + drone is from their actual site. Workers train in the exact environment they'll work in — same tight corridors, same overhead crane, same trench shoring. Gaussian Splatting preserves every detail at photographic resolution.

### Pillar 2 — AI DISPLACED THE DESK. THE TRADES ABSORBED THE WORKFORCE.
*The next wave of construction workers didn't grow up on a jobsite. You need to train them like it.*

The macro context positions this as forward-thinking, not reactive. Companies that build scalable training infrastructure *now* will outcompete those scrambling to onboard at the last minute.

### Pillar 3 — NOT A VIDEO. NOT A GAME. SOMETHING NEW.
*This isn't Unity. This isn't a $60K simulation studio. This is your site, rebuilt in 3D, captured in a day.*

Address the misconception that spatial 3D training = expensive game engine production. A Gaussian Splat is captured on-site in hours, processed into an interactive 3D environment, and deployable to any device.

### Pillar 4 — TRAIN ONCE. DEPLOY EVERYWHERE.
*New hire in Phoenix, crew lead in Denver, safety refresher in Houston — same environment, same standard.*

Multi-site companies can use one splat session to create consistent training across locations. No re-shooting. No regional inconsistency.
